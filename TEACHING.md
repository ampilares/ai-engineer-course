# Teaching and facilitation

Teach one shared problem through increasingly capable systems. Every week should end with evidence another person can inspect. The [course guide](COURSE.md) defines the sequence and [assessments](ASSESSMENTS.md) define the gates.

## Before the cohort

Run the offline labs and verify the worked answers. Rehearse any live OpenClaw, Hermes, Grok Bot, or n8n demonstration in dedicated test accounts. Record the actual environment, versions, account capabilities, and usage costs. Documentary research does not substitute for that rehearsal.

Prepare fictional files for agents separately from evaluator answers. Create fresh held-out variants for assessed capstones. Make authentic demonstration recordings available if account or accessibility constraints prevent a live run; identify the recording date and version.

Ask learners to choose an engineering or owner route and complete the relevant preparation. Pair owners with implementation partners where useful, but assess each learner's understanding and decisions. Agree a usage budget and a review process for real workplace data.

## A two-hour facilitated session

Use 15 minutes to inspect last week's evidence and a common failure; 25 minutes for the concept and worked example; 15 minutes for a live demonstration including a mistake; 45 minutes for paired studio work; 15 minutes for review against acceptance criteria; and five minutes for the next evidence commitment.

Allow asynchronous reading and lab work to fill the weekly time budget. Supply text transcripts or written action records for demonstrations. Avoid making rapid typing, account ownership, or visual-only instructions a hidden prerequisite.

## Weekly facilitation prompts

| Week | Demonstrate | Ask the learner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A fast draft with expensive correction | What counts as complete, and who checks it? |
| 2 | A plausible citation that does not support a claim | Which passage establishes your conclusion? |
| 3 | Duplicate, missing, and wrong-currency rows | Can every source row be accounted for? |
| 4 | A passing test that checks the wrong rule | What independent example would expose the error? |
| 5 | Schema-valid but wrong classification | Which checks address meaning rather than structure? |
| 6 | Stale, draft, and restricted policy records | Which source is effective and authorised for this request? |
| 7 | Crash after the recipient commits an effect | How will you avoid repeating the business action? |
| 8 | A tool call rejected outside the prompt | Where is authority actually enforced? |
| 9 | OpenClaw access denial and stop/restart | What can the connected account and channel do? |
| 10 | Hermes fresh-session memory test | What was actually written, and how can it be corrected? |
| 11 | Grok Bot routine pause and account-shared access | Which comparison variables are hidden? |
| 12 | A high average with a critical boundary failure | Why would this still block the release? |
| 13 | Negative value at low volume | Who owns review, maintenance, and the fallback? |
| 14 | Trace one case through the complete system | Which interface still depends on an undocumented assumption? |
| 15 | Separate first-pass and corrected success | Which denominator makes the conclusion fair? |
| 16 | Another person follows the runbook | Can the process continue safely without its author? |

## Review and feedback

Use the weekly rubric consistently across routes. Engineering evidence includes code, meaningful checks, and traces. Owner evidence includes the configured workflow, acceptance results, reviewer decisions, and operating procedure. Both require working artifacts and independently verified outputs.

Ask learners to explain one rejected AI suggestion and revise a changed case without simply repeating the assistant's answer. Give one strength, one consequential gap, and one specific next experiment. Reassess failed gates after correction rather than averaging them away.

For model-graded work, have a human independently label a sample first. Discuss disagreements and adjust the rubric. Never let the system grade its own permission violations solely through its narrative of what happened.

## Access alternatives and truthful evidence

The offline labs teach interfaces, arithmetic, state, and failure handling. They do not prove live model quality or product compatibility. An unavailable platform can be assessed through official documentation plus authentic instructor evidence, with the selected implementation route demonstrated in depth.

If a learner cannot run a live pilot, accept a clearly labelled simulation and assess the method, limitations, and proposed next validation. Do not award a claim of workplace savings from synthetic data.

## Close the cohort

Have learners stop routines, close test integrations, revoke temporary credentials, and retain only approved evidence. Record practical assessment separately from the site's quiz/self-attestation record. Before a future cohort, recheck [the research register](course/research.md) and apply the [maintenance procedure](course/maintenance.md).
