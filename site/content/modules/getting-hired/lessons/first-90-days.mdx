The final career step is choosing an entry route and using the first ninety days to establish durable trust. Both decisions benefit from explicit goals, evidence, and regular review.

## Three doors in

- **The internal transition**: often the most accessible path. If you already understand an organization's workflows, build a guarded automation for your own team and measure the outcome. This can create production experience without an immediate employer change and may support a formal move into an AI-focused role.
- **The direct hire** is the standard portfolio and interview route. Target teams that ship AI features into production and value evaluation, reliability, and operations. Relevant titles include AI engineer, product-focused ML engineer, AI platform engineer, and software engineer on an AI product team. Evaluate the responsibilities and evidence expected rather than relying on the title alone.
- **The freelance or consulting route**: viable when you can define scope, manage data and security obligations, and measure value. Small organizations often need help with document intake, support triage, or report drafting. A measured result and a credible case study support future work more effectively than a general claim. Agree on value, scope, support, and risk allocation before choosing a pricing model.

## The first 90 days: build trust before ambition

A technically impressive demonstration creates little value if nobody adopts it. Use the first ninety days to learn the environment, deliver one measured pilot, and make the result operable:

- **Days 1–30: map before you build.** Learn the systems, meet the process owners, find where hours bleed (Module 13's discovery, applied internally). Ship something *tiny but real*: a cleanup script, an eval for an existing feature. Small early wins buy patience for bigger ones.
- **Days 31–60: one measured pilot.** Pick a high-volume, low-blast-radius workflow; build the guarded version with a baseline, confidence routing, and a human fallback. Publish honest numbers, including the review-queue rate.
- **Days 61–90: make it durable and legible.** Monitoring, runbook, cost dashboard (Modules 14–16), and a one-page write-up leadership can understand. Then propose the next target from your day-30 map: now with credibility capital to spend.

Throughout, **communicate outcomes together with controls**. A statement such as "reduced cycle time by 80%, measured a 0.4% error rate, and retained human approval for payments above €500" is more informative than a list of technologies.

## Staying an AI engineer

The field will continue to change. Maintain the habits from Module 17: reason from stable primitives, evaluate new techniques on representative data, build complete systems at a manageable scope, and publish clear accounts of what you learned. A consistent record of evidence and judgment compounds over time.
