Suppose an owner says, "I want AI to run support." This is too broad to evaluate. A workable first version is: "Given one ticket, propose its category and whether a human must review it; save a draft without sending a response." It names an input, an output, an authority boundary, and a finish condition.

The ten fictional Harbour timings total 120 staff minutes. Their average is 12 minutes per case. Do not translate this directly into a savings claim. A real baseline needs a defined start and end, representative cases, and a record of rework. A single easy ticket processed in three minutes tells you little about the next week's mix.

Keep interruptions distinguishable from work. Define "complete" before starting the timer: a draft without checking is not comparable with a fully reviewed manual response.

Rank opportunities using five questions: Is the task frequent? Is successful output observable? Is the necessary information available and authorised? Can mistakes be caught before affecting others? Is someone responsible for exceptions? The ranking is a discussion aid, not a mathematically objective score. A rare task with serious consequences may deserve a different process despite an impressive demo.
