Evals tell you how the system performs on known inputs before shipping. **Observability** tells you what's happening with real users right now, and gives you the raw material (traces) to debug the failures you didn't anticipate. Offline evals + online monitoring form one loop, and production failures feed the eval set. That loop is the whole methodology.

## Traces: the debugging superpower

A **trace** is the complete record of one request through your system: every step, in order, with inputs, outputs, and timings:

```text
trace 8f3a · user question 14:02:31 · total 6.4s · $0.011
 ├─ retrieval: query → 5 chunks (ids, scores)          320ms
 ├─ prompt build: 2,840 tokens (template v14)           2ms
 ├─ model call: claude-sonnet, temp 0.2 → 480 tokens  5.9s
 ├─ validation: schema OK, 2 citations                  1ms
 └─ response → user; feedback: 👎
```

A negative user rating becomes diagnosable when you can read the trace from top to bottom. Determine whether retrieval supplied the wrong chunks or generation produced a poor answer from good chunks. Without a trace, the complaint lacks enough context for a targeted fix. With a trace, it can become a classified failure and a new evaluation case. Capture traces from the beginning, with appropriate privacy controls, instead of waiting for the first unexplained incident.

Two hygiene rules: **redact secrets and sensitive personal data** before storage (traces are a data liability: Module 15 returns to this), and **version-stamp everything** (prompt version, model ID, retrieval config) so a trace answers "what exactly produced this?"

## Metrics and alerts: the dashboard that matters

Four families, per feature:

- **Quality proxies:** Track validation failures, abstention, citation coverage, user feedback, and human correction rates in review queues. Corrections are especially valuable when they are sampled, reviewed, and added to a versioned evaluation dataset.
- **Latency**: p50 and **p95/p99** (means hide the pain users remember), split by stage.
- **Cost**: tokens and € per request and per day, with **budget alerts** (the difference between "unusual Tuesday" and "surprise invoice").
- **Reliability**: error rates by type: timeouts, rate limits, refusals, truncations.

Alert on *changes*, not just absolutes: correction rate doubling week-over-week is your earliest **drift** signal: inputs shifting under the model (new document format, new customer segment, provider model update). Drift is Module 5's promise arriving on schedule; monitoring is how you catch it before your users do.

## Instrument for diagnosis and privacy

Use one correlation ID across the user request, retrieval, model calls, tools, queue transitions, approvals, and final outcome. Each span should record operation name, start/end, status, model/config version, token counts, retry count, and safe identifiers. Apply redaction before telemetry leaves the process, restrict trace access, and set retention by data sensitivity. Observability is not permission to create a second uncontrolled copy of every prompt.

Monitor service health, model-system quality, safety, and business outcomes together. A quality drop may come from a source parser, traffic mix, stale index, or review backlog rather than the model. Segment and correlate. Set alerts on actionable symptoms with owners and runbooks; noisy alerts teach teams to ignore the system.

Feedback is biased: users rate unusual failures more than routine success, and thumbs-up is not a gold label. Join feedback with sampled audits and downstream outcomes. Keep training/eval inclusion as a reviewed pipeline with consent and provenance.
