Between a model's raw internal scores and the probabilities it reports sits one small, ubiquitous function: **softmax**. Understanding it (and the logarithms and exponentials it's built from) turns several course-long mysteries (temperature, logits, "cross-entropy loss") into plain mechanics.

## Exponentials and logarithms in two paragraphs

**Exponential growth** multiplies: 2, 4, 8, 16… Each step scales by a factor. Exponentials turn small input differences into huge output differences: `e^5` is ~148 but `e^10` is ~22,000.

**Logarithms** are the inverse: they count *how many multiplications* got you somewhere, compressing huge ranges into small ones (log₁₀ of 1,000,000 is just 6). Two practical consequences you'll use: multiplying probabilities (which shrink toward zero catastrophically fast) becomes *adding* their logs (which is why losses and likelihoods live in log-space) and quantities spanning many magnitudes (costs, token counts, probabilities) are best compared on log scales.

## Softmax: scores → probabilities

A model's final layer produces a raw score per vocabulary token (**logits**) which can be any numbers at all: `[5.1, 2.3, -1.0, …]`. Probabilities must be positive and sum to 1. Softmax is the converter:

```text
softmax(zᵢ) = e^(zᵢ) / Σⱼ e^(zⱼ)
```

Exponentiate every score (making them positive and *amplifying gaps*), then divide by the total (making them sum to 1). You have already spent quality time with softmax without the name: the sampling widget's bars *are* softmax outputs, and its temperature slider divides the logits before the exponential:

<SamplingSimulator />

Now the slider is transparent: **temperature T divides every logit.** Small T → gaps between scores get amplified enormously by the exponential → the top token swallows the distribution (greedy). Large T → gaps shrink toward zero → near-uniform randomness. Temperature never adds knowledge; it repartitions the same scores.

## Cross-entropy, demystified

Training (Module 6) needs to score how far a predicted distribution is from the observed target. For a single correct class, **cross-entropy** reduces to the negative log-probability assigned to that class. Assigning the correct next token probability `0.9` produces a loss of about `0.105`; assigning it `0.001` produces a loss of about `6.9`. The loss therefore penalizes confident wrong predictions much more strongly than uncertain but correct ones. In a batch or sequence, training aggregates this quantity across examples or tokens.

Vocabulary unlocked: **logits** (raw scores), **softmax** (scores converted to probabilities), **temperature** (a logit-scaling parameter), **log-probabilities** (a numerically stable form of likelihood), and **cross-entropy** (a loss based on the negative log-probability of the target). These terms form one connected family that appears throughout machine learning.
