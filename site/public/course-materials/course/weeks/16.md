# Week 16: Demonstrate, defend, and hand over

The final week proves that someone other than the builder can understand and operate the system. The deliverable is a working process, its evidence, and an adoption decision. A polished demonstration cannot replace missing evaluation or operational control.

## 1. Assemble a traceable evidence pack

Organise the submission so a reviewer can move from claim to evidence. Start with the problem, intended users, scope, and decision. Link to the baseline, setup, version record, evaluation inputs, results, boundary tests, runbook, cost model, and user guide. Keep source files and generated outputs distinguishable.

For each headline metric, include definition, numerator, denominator, measurement window, source, and limitation. "90% accurate" is incomplete without knowing which cases and what counted as correct. "Saves five hours" is incomplete without review effort and case volume. An honest limitation makes the evidence easier to use.

The course's practical rubric weights weekly work at 30%, platform comparison at 15%, capstone at 45%, and demonstration/handover at 10%. The website's automatically graded module checks measure conceptual knowledge separately. A site completion certificate and self-attestation do not establish that an independent reviewer graded the full practical portfolio.

### Worked adoption decision

"Adopt for internal draft preparation for one team, with mandatory review and a monthly cost cap. Do not enable external sending. Reassess after fifty additional cases and after any permission or model change." This is more actionable than "the AI works." A revise decision names the blocking issue, owner, and new evidence required. A stop decision names why continued implementation is not justified.

## 2. Demonstrate success, failure, blocked action, and recovery

Plan a five-minute demonstration. Spend about one minute on the problem and baseline, one minute on normal operation, one minute on a failure and its handling, one minute on a blocked action and recovery, and one minute on results and the recommendation. Prepare the evidence beforehand, but disclose whether a run is live or recorded.

Choose a failure relevant to the project. A missing document should produce an unresolved question rather than an invented answer. A revoked permission should prevent retrieval. A changed action payload should require renewed approval. An interrupted workflow should resume or enter reconciliation according to its documented limits.

Have a second person follow the runbook. They should identify pending work, pause new execution, inspect the last version, restore the approved configuration, and know whom to contact. Record where your documentation was insufficient and correct it before final submission.

### Defend the design

Be ready to explain why you selected the model or platform, why an agent was or was not useful, which facts must be retrieved, how tool authority is enforced, how you measured human effort, and which alternative you rejected. Explain one thing the AI suggested that you changed. You do not need to memorise every library API; you do need to understand your system's behaviour and limits.

If a reviewer asks for a new requirement, distinguish a small supported variation from a change outside the evaluated scope. Do not improvise a production claim during the demonstration. Record the new case and decide what must be tested before accepting it.

## 3. Final studio, assessment, and next steps

### Engineering route

1. Reproduce the final candidate from its documented setup and version record.
2. Run the agreed acceptance and boundary checks. Preserve the output and any unresolved limitations.
3. Demonstrate the four behaviours: success, failure, blocked action, and recovery.
4. Hand over the repository, configuration instructions, runbook, and maintenance ownership.
5. Explain the next improvement you would test and the evidence that would justify it.

### Business-owner route

1. Verify the workflow, staff procedure, review responsibilities, and manual fallback are usable by another person.
2. Present the measured or clearly simulated results and volume-sensitive business case.
3. Demonstrate how staff identify uncertainty, reject an action, and stop the workflow.
4. Name the operational owner, budget owner, and reviewer for future changes.
5. Choose adopt, revise, or stop and communicate the scope of that decision.

### Completion criteria

Submit every item in [the capstone checklist](../../CAPSTONE.md). A passing practical result requires at least 70% overall and all mandatory progression gates. A critical unauthorised-action failure cannot be offset by attractive outputs or high marks elsewhere. Self-directed learners should ask an independent peer to review the evidence; otherwise label the practical assessment as self-reviewed.

Keep learning by investigating a measured limitation. Use [the optional extensions](../extensions.md) for forecasting, model internals, advanced retrieval, adaptation, local inference, multiple agents, or voice. Select one experiment, compare it with the strongest simple baseline, and preserve the same discipline of evidence, boundaries, and operational ownership.
